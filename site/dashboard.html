      Exposure matrix: which catalysts move which assets

      Analyst rating of transmission strength, 0 none – 3 primary channel. Hover a cell for the mechanism. These ratings feed the exposure index on the Signals tab.

      Method and caveats

      1. Exposure index. For each asset class, the sum over live catalysts of (analyst risk score 1–5 × exposure rating 0–3 to the catalyst's channel), scaled so the most exposed asset = 100. It measures how much geopolitical risk an asset should carry, not its direction.
      2. Pricing. The asset's gauge as a percentile of its one-year range, inverted where a high reading means complacency (TAIEX, Bovespa). For yields, spreads, volatility and USD crosses, high = risk priced.
      3. Gap and thresholds. Gap = exposure − pricing; ±25 triggers a signal, ±50 is high conviction. Thresholds are heuristics, not optimized.
      4. Confirmation. A positive gap becomes "Under-priced" only if the gauge moved in its risk-priced direction on average and in at least half of the 2026 escalations and, for the six series with history, also in the 2019–2025 analogs. One sample agreeing = "Mixed" (Watch). This keeps the engine from recommending hedges that have not worked (gold this year) or whose behaviour has flipped (the 10-year).
      5. Vehicle. Where an implied-vol index exists (OVX for oil, GVZ for gold, MOVE for rates), the spot percentile is compared with the implied-vol percentile: options are preferred when implied vol is ≥ 25 points cheaper, outright when ≥ 25 richer.
      6. Desk reads. Rule-based sentences built from each desk's metrics — e.g. Rates labels the curve bear-steepening when the 10-year and 3m10y have both risen since 27 Feb; Credit flags decompression when CCC–BBB widened more than 25 bp in a month; Volatility calls the VIX curve in contango below 0.95 and inverted above 1.
      7. Momentum, oil beta, scenarios. 20-day change ÷ (daily vol × √20); 60-day beta to Brent's daily move; scenario-weighted 5-day move from 2026 event-study averages and analyst probabilities.
      8. Evidence. In-sample: VIX rose over the following 10 days in — of windows when the gap was ≥ 50 vs — when it was below 25.
      9. Fed tracker. Plumbing statuses compare each micro-indicator with written thresholds (see the monitor). Fedspeak tone is the average analyst stance score (−2 to +2) of dated communications over 30 days. Scenario moves = assumed 2-year shock × each asset's beta to the 2-year with Brent held constant (two-factor regression on one year of daily data), cross-checked against Fed-event days only.
      10. Shock lab. Event shocks are measured average 5-day moves (2026 events or 2019–25 analogs). Factor shocks use ordered sensitivities: the 2-year and Brent enter one regression together (so a Fed shock carries its usual equity reaction), and the S&P 500 is added third, so the equity shock is a sell-off beyond what rates and oil explain. Combined scenarios multiply probabilities as if independent and add a 5-day geopolitical bundle to a same-day Fed reaction.
      11. Caveats. One year of daily data (desk series from 3 Nov 2025) and small event samples; percentiles use the full window (look-ahead); overlapping backtest windows; no transaction costs; tanker equity, index-level implied vol and five-print breakevens are proxies for vendor data.
