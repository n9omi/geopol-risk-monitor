    Method and caveats

    1. Exposure index. For each asset class, sum over live catalysts of (risk score × exposure rating to the catalyst's channel); the most exposed asset is scaled to 100. It measures how much geopolitical risk an asset should carry, not its direction.
    2. Pricing. The asset's gauge as a one-year percentile, inverted where a high reading means complacency (TAIEX). For yields, spreads, volatility and USD-crosses, high = risk priced.
    3. Gap and thresholds. Gap = exposure − pricing; ±25 triggers a signal, ±50 is high conviction. Thresholds are heuristics, not optimized.
    4. Confirmation. A positive gap only becomes "Under-priced" if the gauge moved in its risk-priced direction on average and in at least half of past Middle East escalations. This stops the engine from recommending hedges that have not worked (gold this year).
    5. Momentum, oil beta, scenarios. 20-day change ÷ (daily vol × √20); 60-day beta to Brent's daily move; scenario-weighted 5-day move from event-study averages and analyst probabilities.
    6. Caveats. One year of daily data and a handful of events; percentiles use the full window (look-ahead within the sample); no transaction costs or out-of-sample test. The next step is a rolling-window backtest of the gap signal against forward VIX and Brent returns.
